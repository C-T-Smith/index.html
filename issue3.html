Issue #3

The Apple Tree
A Magazine of Fiction, Science, and a little more Fiction!

Kaldran unlocked his case with a click. The hill he stood upon looked out across a rolling expanse of both fields and trees, almost seeming an endless mass that stretched on forever. Tucked away on the top of the hill and surrounded by waves of carefree waving grass there was a tree. A large and ancient apple tree that rose up with a quiet authority, though unleafed. One that held a low rumble of simple power. It was a magnificent and potent tree, but that wasn’t why Kaldran was here. Lifting the lid, he reverently took out an instrument. It was a sleek violin, glowing a natural red in the shade of the tree. The sun glinted through the branches briefly and lit up its surface. Pulling out his bow he slowly tightened and rozened it, preparing for the music to come. After a quick tuning he set it back down again and sat down in silence. Mindlessly he plucked a few notes that mirrored his aimless concentration. The grass waved in response and slowly began moving contrary to the slight breeze blowing past it.

In a relaxed stance Kaldran stood up and deftly picked up the bow. Laying it on the string he poured his heart into the instrument and played as things began to happen. Emotion welled up with the music, a dragging, longing feeling that required everything in its vicinity to stop and remember. The breeze chilled and grass began to bend away from him. Sadness pervaded the song at an impossibly deep level. They were inseparable. Suddenly but somehow correctly his song changed. The song became furious. The grass moved in fear, the tree shook. Kaldran took every mistake he’d ever made, every decision he’d ever regretted, and shoved them through the song. Clouds erupted through the sky and quickly grew dark and ominous. The sun disappeared as everything turned dark and ominous. He slowed down. Exhausted for the moment, he moved into a sort of sullen and more frightening story. A song that followed all the drama of Dvorak’s ninth with all the sadness of Schindler’s list.

Eyes shut, tightly Kaldran moved back towards his terrifying regretful stave. As the song deepened, thunder began to rumble and the wind increased. Blowing hard now, the wind pushed the ancient apple to and fro. Firmly planted the tree’s roots kept it safely mounted while its branches whipped around chaotically. The thunder grew louder in synchronization with Kaldran’s heartbreaking melody but the wind did not touch him. He remained as firmly planted as the tree but as lost and angry as the storm. He played lower and the rocks themselves began floating slowly up from the ground. Pebbles that lined the path up the hill began to hover and rain began to fall in large fat droplets. Captured by his heart-wrenching anthem the rain turned hail but never once touched him.

Kaldran's fingers danced upwards as he approached a higher octave. His lower notes turned shrill and lightning burst from the laden clouds. Light flashed, lighting his face in a stark and terrifying picture. He shut his eyes even tighter and his playing wobbled tremulously. The lightning struck somewhere nearby, but Kaldran was protected in his bubble of anguished inspiration. Tears streaked across his face and his fingers diminished in speed, regaining their slow and utterly painful mourn. His soul stretched to near breaking. He poured every ounce of life into this attempt to make something beautiful. More beautiful than summer, more terrible than the storm, more majestic than a full moon, it was a lament to shake nations themselves.

Fully devoting himself to the music, fog drenched itself over the surrounding landscape, obscuring the hills beyond. Kaldran searched deeply, plumbing the depths of man for any sense of hope, any glimmer of peace, any small light of comfort, but found none to fuel his melody. Vast booms echoed across the sky and the hail grew larger while the rain somehow increased. Blasting wind shook the hill and reminded the world of every injustice while lightning splintered the sky, calling to memory every broken oath.

A rogue note appeared in the ballad. Frustrated, Kaldran played harder, trying desperately to perfect it. Again it appeared. Again he moved into playing more furiously, thinking to beat the major key back with the minor, but nothing worked. Soon notes that he hadn’t commanded made their way across his instrument and light appeared through the sky. A glowing, golden, glittering break of pure and unhindered hope. Kaldran searched himself again for the notes required to make such an appearance, but he was empty of all such joy. Attempting to keep his playing pure and true, he worked even harder. He bashed the majestic melody with the strongest ensemble he could come up with but nothing could help. He reached for drama but it was slowly turned from being somber into a mighty piece of joy. The song admitted its player’s complete and utter lack of peace while simultaneously acknowledging its creator’s magnificent glory.

While Kaldran was left fighting for control of his music the clouds broke even further. The golden light landed like a spotlight on the ancient tree and prevented the player from having any choice but to look at it. Broken splinters of wood splayed across the top of the tree and burn marks traced a jagged path down its middle. A large gouge had been ripped out from the inside, exposing rings of dead heartwood and its roots were nearly torn up from the base. In defeated revulsion Kaldran scrambled back and tried desperately to pack his instrument away and out of sight.

Scared now, he found that he couldn’t release his grip on his violin. Instead he was left staring at what he had done with no way to hide. So he tried to run. Scrabbling back to his feat, he quickly realized that his feet were planted as firmly as the tree had been, a realization that only made his tears fall faster. His arm raised his instrument to his shoulder and he warbled out a broken tune. It was nothing compared to the storm that was raging around him, nothing compared to the light that streamed directly around the tree. But that didn’t stop whoever it was that was playing for him. His fingers grew steadier and his arm more confident until there was a melody that declared peace. Peace to the storm around him, calm to the wind that shook the hill, rest to the rocks that had yet to cease floating, and healing to the tree that was undone. As the clouds dispersed and the breeze returned the song grew joyous. The sun reunited with the sky and the fog was completely gone, drying everything out and revealing the hills beyond. Light took up its position again and summer reasserted itself. The tree, however, was still blown over.

It was about this point where Kaldran reasserted himself. While he had let the music follow its course freely, he decided that he could step in now. Directing his song towards the tree, he watched as its roots climbed, extending themselves, in search for the ground. They dove deeply into the earth and Kaldran could hear rumbling coming from the ground as rocks below were split and pushed aside, making way for the tree’s steadfast roots. Next he turned his attention to the tree’s side. His fingers moved faster now, a lovely parody of the terrible storm from before. Sapwood edged its way around the gaping gouge and carefully sealed the tree. Bark soon followed and after that the ancient tree gave a tremendous groan, and buds burst across its branches. Leaves came forth and blossoms ringed themselves around like a crown of beautiful flowers.

Kaldran’s fingers slowed and the song came to an end. It was a lovely midsummer day when he packed his violin back away in his case and loosened his bow. He followed the stony path down but found himself stopping at the bottom to look behind him. On the top of the hill stood an ancient and blooming apple tree, one that had outlived many generations and would outlive many more.
