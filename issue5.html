Issue #5

The Frozen Wanderer
A Magazine of Fiction, Science, and a little more Fiction!

The wind whistled loudly around the frozen man’s ears. He’d been wandering for…what felt like ever. Ice clung to his loose brown hair tinkling slightly as he shuffled over the frozen white expanse in front of him. Moving steadily north for three days now he had long since realized that he was not made for such deathly temperatures. His feet had long ago given up the feeling of warmth and each of his steps was announced by a sudden exhale of frozen breath. As he crested yet another dune he looked behind himself–marked by a stiff crack in the neck of his coat–and saw the winding footsteps of a man possessed. His very being emanated his will to not survive, but to succeed. Death was a problem only if it came before his mission’s success.

Returning to his work the intrepid wanderer slowly began his descent, struggling for footholds on the loosely set snow. Hands out for balance, the cold came creeping in. Though his fingers were long numb from the frigid temperatures his core was surviving. The man pulled his balaclava higher over his face, neglecting to notice that this exposed his neck to the wind. Every minute he felt as if he lost another inch of his body. Stumbling forward he moved past this thought and covered another foot of ground. Through the bottom, up the top. Over the hill, down the next. Days had passed. His mission was imperative. What was it? Broken into steps his plan looked like “cross the snowy dessert”. Broken up into more steps it looked like “cross the next dune” or “just one more step”.

In all actuality he couldn’t recall his original plan. No matter. He trusted his former self much more than his frozen version. Cresting the hill he looked back again. His trail, however cold and incomprehensible, remained straight. A literal depiction of his inner self. Man was not made to be a liar and no aspect of nature was going to rebut the promise he had made so many years ago. The cold crept in. His fire burned in reply. Nothing could stop him from keeping his oath, save the man himself. The snow gave out from beneath him. Or maybe his leg did, but it had the same effect. He went tumbling down, snow everywhere and his sense of direction nowhere. The ground finally rose to meet him and enthusiastically made its presence known. Ouch. Something had snapped. Righting himself, he laboriously checked one ankle and then the next. One was instantly swollen, the pain matched only by the cold. No matter.

Lonely but steadfast he stood up to keep a promise. He would walk whatever path was given of him in order to reach his goal, to fulfill his purpose. Even if he couldn't. The impossibility of the latter situation was not important to him. Five steps, ten steps, the ice coated the inside of his sleeves now, not just the outside. His hair was a frozen mass not a wind chyme anymore. Hats were useless, goggles more so. What were gloves anyway? Cresting the next hill he could have vaguely seen a small town. A stalwart town, not willing to admit that maybe life was better in warmer climates. Nope. This town has stood for many generations and it will continue to do so though the years slip by. In essence it was a cold, cold, town. But it was his destination whether he saw it or not.

Faltering down the slope, he did not look up. His feet moved of their own accord, picking a path that avoided……..what? All the hills were the same, every slope exact, no path showed any sign of variance. No path led ahead. Just a hill. The cold crept in. His fire dimmed. No! His chest begged, his heart ached to finish the quest. Stop! His mind yearned to be done, to end. His very being was torn in two as one half slid to the ground, fire gone. The wind picked up but he couldn’t feel it anymore. Crying out in anger his soul ignited. What once had dimmed came roaring back to strength. He walked, not once looking back at the snow that quickly hid a still, frozen form.

Gone was the cold, gone was the doubt. All that remained was a man running to fulfill his purpose. Quickening his pace he jogged. Then ran. Time was too short to speak of the flowing white hills that surrounded him, pristine in the early morning sun. Neither the shimmering delicate snow beneath him, nor the raging red clouds above caught his attention. All that mattered was the oath he had sworn so, so, long ago, it seemed like another life.

The town appeared ahead in majestic beauty. His town. His promise. His eyes, formerly deadened, were now alive. Running up to the gate he slowed, suddenly terrified. He lowered his gaze, crystal eyes shifting to the ground. He lifted his eyes, prepared to speak and only then realized that the gate had opened and a man was walking out to greet him. The fire inside the man was no flame compared to the one walking toward him. No, the fire presented was immense, an infinite power. The man shifted his eyes again. What was he to this omnipotent blaze? He was the snow, the cold that crept in with every breath, the ice that chilled the thickest glove. But he was part of the oath. Oaths had been pledged and debts had been paid. No debt can be forgiven, only paid, a truth the man understood well. The fire before him grew with an infectious and irresistible power. The man’s flame grew. His debt was paid, an oath fulfilled. He knelt. He had been redeemed. He could never repay the man that paid his debt.

“Well done my good and faithful servant.”
