Issue #6

The Hearing of Uther Wyrmstyle
A Magazine of Fiction, Science, and a little more Fiction!

The supermarket in question was entirely devoid of the interesting little yellow fascination called the marshmallow peep. Gone. Split off the planet. Ah well, Mrs. Wyrmstyle would just have to go without. Or, as was more likely the case, her guests would, as she never personally indulged in the sweet herself. Uther sighed and brushed a hand through his hair. Best get it over with. He tossed a bag of little white puffy sugarbowls into his cart and headed back the way he had come. Where were the real ones? Not that he could actually stomach them, but his life would be much easier if he could return to Enderby with a completed list of grocery items.

As he retraced his steps Uther scanned the aisles. There was no being too careful, he was thinking to himself when his cart forcefully came to a halt. To his left in aisle four there was a man wearing his exact peacoat and hairstyle. Only, the hair was a bit more ragged and the trainers newer. He continued to stare until he realized the foolishness of it. He’d gotten his at the local thrift, there was no reason to say this other man couldn’t have done the same. Right as he was turning back to leave the man glanced up. Before the man could notice he had been the subject of Uther’s mind for a good minute Uther snapped his head straight forward.

Two aisles later, with the wheel to his cart squeaking the whole way, Uther arrived at the checkout and began laboring to unload various little parcels and packages, whistling to himself in a not unpleasant way.

“Good day, is it?” asked the checkout lady. She was somewhere in between the state of being overly large and having ceased to care. In other words life hadn’t been pleasant for her recently and the dwarfing effect she had on those around her announced it to the world.

“Large!” Uther pointed out before he could clamp his lips shut. Fortunately the only thing to escape was a muffled “lug”.

“Oy? I beg your pardon?” The woman threatened, hand stalled in the air and clutching a frozen hammock. “What did you say?”

“I-” his eyes darted to the hammock in her fist. Dents had appeared where her fingers were and the frost coating the ham had all but disappeared. “Labrador.” he said belatedly but there was no chance now, he had to finish. “Labrador of a day.”

The lady eyed him up and down, taking in his disintegrating trainers and mediocre peacoat. The quality of a man was not determined by the clothes he wore but by his success in business, Uther’s grandfather had always said. Which was interesting considering that his grandfather had also been marooned in Leicester by a passing teaser intent on the practical joke that had brought about his son Herman Wyrmstyle’s existence and through him with a Tavernkeeper from Enderby, Uther himself. The tavern was nice enough, Herman had thought upon his arrival at the dim grime covered shack. The beer, really, is what he meant, because five drinks later the only thing he could remember was the sight of a tall woman with eyes that looked like Hraffleberries. Herman never could properly explain to Uther what a Hraffleberry was, only that he ought to bless his lucky stars they looked like his mother’s eyes.

“Labrador?” The woman enunciated slowly. The thunderous booming of her voice jumped Uther from his stupor. He let out a squeak but she seemed to accept his cover as truth. “Labrador of a day it is, sir. Though, my gran might claim it’s more of a Beagle today.” She gazed out through a window longingly but she knew there was nothing for it. “Anything you couldn’t find?”

“Not at all, I’m afraid.” Then he paused, glancing back up. “Well actually, there is. Funny little yellow-orange things. Peeps. ‘You know where I could find ‘em?”

“mmmhhh-hhhhhhhmmmm,” she sighed. To Uther, who had inherited none of his mother’s height, the air that blasted from her voluminous chest was of hurricanic proportions. “Off in the back right. In with the lollies. Can’t miss it.”

Could miss it. Quite easily, in fact, but the image of the crushed frozen ham played itself back in his mind. It wasn’t worth it.

“Be quick!” She called over his quickly retreating shoulder. “I ‘aven’t got all day!”

This time it was Uther’s own trainers that squeaked across the grocery floor but the sound grated across his nerves all the same. Isle four passed in a blur, reminding him to slow down. There was no point tripping to death over a loose tile. Mrs. Wyrmstyle was bound to have a fit if he not only refused to bring back a proper serving of peeps but moreover refused to even bring himself back, the host of the party she was supposed to be conducting. Various short fantasies of him explaining the situation to her each with greater and greater injuries but the same result came flickered across his mind.

There was a slight jolt as Uther’s cart collided with the milk stand off to his left. He’d come full circle and was nearly on the far side of aisle four. With a start he glanced up from his shoes to see a man with a startlingly blue complexion.

“Mr. Wyrmstyle?” asked the blue skinned man in a vaguely yorkshire-ish accent. “Mr. Uther Wyrmstyle?” When he nodded the blue skinned man blinked and continued. “Would it be correct to suggest that one Mr. Herman Wyrmstyle is not around any longer?”

“Eh? Wha- we-...” he stammered helpfully. “As in gone? Not as far as I’m aware, not exactly as such, no.”

The man looked taken aback, as if he hadn’t expected to be contradicted. He covered remarkably well though and continued.

“Well in that case,” went the vaguely Yorkshire accent, “Where might we find him?”

“We? What-”

“I,” the blue skinned man interrupted. “Where can I find him?”

“The churchyard, I should think. Can’t expect anyone would want to have robbed him. Didn’t leave me a penny, can’t imagine someone would dig him up. Unless of course he was exhumed, I guess. Or, possibly if he was dug up by critters, though there aren’t many here in Leicester,...” His rambling came to a halt as the man’s skin grew a deeper shade of blue. “...Unless of course that's what you meant by gone,” he finished. “Then yes, as you say,... actually, I haven't the foggiest. What were you going to say?”

“You are then, as I say,” he emphasized the I in a tired but determined manner, “the last living descendant of your grandfather, Gruzwalkbourk Wyrmstyle. Your father is dead and you have no siblings.”

Uther glanced shyly over at the milk for inspiration. Yes, he was. There was no way of getting around it. Why this blue skinned man was interested was another question entirely. And why blue? Was he from Hollywood or something? An American. That would explain the phony accent and the blue skin. Yes, an American was all too likely, and on one of those prank shows too, where innocent British citizens were made to perform for the entertainment of the low brow Yankee masses. All too likely. Uther decided he had better just get it over with.

“Yes, I am, as you say, I am the last living descendant of my grandfather Gruz…Gruzzalky.. Oh, just Grandpa Wyrmstyle! Get it over with! Bring out the cameras, you ignoramus nitwits!” Uther sagged, his peacoat deflating beneath his yielding shoulders.

“No cameras, Mr. Wyrmstyle. Just this.” He held out a slip of yellowing transcript paper. “You are to accompany me to Argabuthin for a hearing.”

“Hearing? I shall do no such thing. I’ll have you know I’m busy at the moment, and have no time to head off to gosh knows where while my wife struggles at home to host a party of twelve. And one without a proper spread no less! No thank you.”

With this he made his way off to the middle of aisle four and didn’t even remark on how quickly the yellowish orange candies popped right out to him now. With a quick snatch he deposited them inside his coat next to his spare pocket handkerchief and headed towards the grocer waiting for him.

“I can’t say that I was asking, Mr. Wyrmstyle. In fact, I can’t say that I care whether you choose to go or not. I can, however, happily inform you that you will be leaving with me in” -he glanced neatly at his digital watch- “exactly thirty seconds. Boys, retrieve him for me.”

Uther, who had stopped dead in his tracks at the mention of anyone “asking” for his opinion, finally looked back to the blue skinned man who interrupted his shopping. More importantly, he looked back at the man’s blue companions who were brandishing wicked looking guns with the word “RizoZapp” stenciled along their short stubby barrels in a thoroughly intimidating manner. In addition to the guns each gunman had a bald blue head with various bony ridges protruding just slightly from the simmering blue skin covered skull.

There was no waiting to be taken. Uther belted out a squeak and dashed into a run. He made it just past the tantalizing selection of Cadbury Boost bars before getting tackled to the floor. Something cold and metallic was shoved roughly into the small of his back despite the fact that he was already down. A second later his arms were pinned back and his wrists cuffed.

“Good. We can get a move on. Not five seconds to spare, gentlemen. Right on schedule. Rupert?” he said to somebody somewhere, “take us up in three, two, one…” the man dissolved before he could finish, leaving behind nothing but a startled customer who had only caught a glimpse of a scene that would doubtless become a news story the very next day containing no fewer than five abductions and an army of invading alien hostiles.
