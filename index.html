The Man in the Grey Suit

It was a warm sunny day as Michael ambled lazily down the sidewalk of a bustling City thoroughfare. Nearly hitting his head on a quaint brown sign above him that read “café dúmon”, he turned to his left and entered the building advertised. The door clinked shut with a cheery ring of a bell and Michael was immediately met with the warm atmosphere of the cosy establishment. Admittedly a small building, the cafè made great use of its space. Four small dual person tables lined the windows facing the street and an additional two the wall perpendicular the roadside. Further inside sat the cafè’s actual work station, lined with beans of different roasts and creams of various questionable formulas. Michael strode confidently forward and peered at the menu, perusing it for anything worthy of note. A moment later he was greeted by the friendly face of the young woman presumably in charge of the counter.

“How’s your day been?” she inquired in a vaguely New York accent. She couldn’t have been more than nineteen, but she carried herself with a confidence that said she knew what she was doing and how to stay calm when somebody ordered a large iced mocha, half-caf, comprised of almond milk with foam on the top, light ice, double blended with just a touch more cocoa than normal and cold but not too cold, lest they get brain freeze. In other words she was young but not new to her job.

“My day has been fine, thank you.” replied Michael cheerily. “Anything special today or should I just go with my usual?”

“We’ve got a pumpkin spice latté if you're interested?”

“It's not fall yet, is it?” he asked somewhat skeptically.

“Jess thought it might bring in more business.” She explained, tossing her head back in the direction of another woman who was addressing one of the coffee machines in a determined manner.

“I’ll just take my regular then. Thanks though.”

“No problem. One boring old lattè coming up.” She continued, teasingly, as she started the transaction.

A few moments later Michael was sitting at his favorite table in the corner and resting his head on the whitewashed brick wall that made up the building, coffee in hand. Oh, the joy of a well run caf—wait, was that guy looking at him weirdly? Michael anchored his feet back on the floor, all four seat legs returning to flat stability. He lent forward, took a sip from his coffee, and peered unobtrusively at the man. He was a short fellow complete with an egg shaped head and magnificent moustache. The suit he was wearing was a stylish grey color yet spoke neither of flamboyant wealth nor of bare poverty. The face ... .well the face displayed a keen intelligence and – their eyes made contact and Michael quickly attempted to cover by scanning the room with his eyes. If all went well the man would simply assume that Michael was looking around the room curiously.

A scraping noise emanated from somewhere off to his left and a second later the man in the grey suit stood and walked the short distance over to Michael. Michael looked up from his coffee to glance at the intruder. The man sat down, twiddled his bow tie expectantly and waited. A moment later he changed his mind and spoke.

“I believe, monsieur, you have something for me…?”

French? Thought Michael. He was about to explain that in fact, he had no idea who the man was and therefore nothing for him but was prevented when the Barista waltzed over to deliver a steaming cup of something light brown.

“Ah, thank you, madame.” The man said with a distinctly polite french accent. Turning to Michael again he continued. “My name, as you may perhaps have guessed is M. —”

“Wait, are you French?” Interrupted Michael rudely.

“No. I am Belgian. I believe we said in our correspondence that you would be wearing a flat brown coat over a grey vest with an orange pocket protector. Additionally you would be carrying a pair of reading spectacles underneath your velvet green bowler hat.” Finishing his declaration the man proceeded to look over him for clarification.

“Ah, you see,..” was all he could manage before the Belgian continued. Indeed Michael was wearing a relatively green bowler hat, brown overcoat, grey vest, and even an orange pocket protector, each item a decision he was starting to regret at the moment.

“très bien!” the man declared, clearly having reached a very different conclusion from Michael’s. “Down to business then. I simply must know whether or not the gardener was at work around three o’clock yesterday afternoon.”

“I, uh,..I don’t have a gardener.” Michael was able to stutter in answer.

“Humour, mon ami, is not suited for such a crucial point. There is nothing hilarious about Murder.” The strange little man said the last word in a distinctly distasteful manner. “There is nothing hilarious about any crime. No. Was the gardener working at the manor at three o’clock of July twenty-fifth?”

Was this some kind of code? What did the man want from him? Michael decided that rather than worry about giving the wrong answer, he was simply going to tell the man the truth and avoid all possible misconceptions of working hours.

“That’s what I meant, there is no gardener. Let alone one who was working yesterday at three o’clock in a busy manner or even a lazy manner. Not that I would keep a lazy gardener mind you.”

“C'est pas vrai! The very idea had not occurred to me!” The grey suited man’s hands shot to his egg-shaped head, cradling his mind as if to keep from getting blown apart. “And you are sure?”

“Quite sure. I think I would know about having a gardener.”

“En effet, my friend, en effet. But what does it mean? If there is no gar…” His voice died down until it was no more than a whispered thought. Following his silence his head shot up, eyes hardened with a determined light. “Merci, mon ami. You have no idea how helpful you have been. I will contact you further if the need arises.” With that he set his drink aside and jumped to his feat. Preening his moustache, he headed out the door and down the way he had originally come.

Michael sat confused, a befuddled look spread across his face. He sipped from his coffee in hopes that it would bring some light onto his situation. What had just happened? One sip clearly wasn’t enough so he accompanied it with a second. Seeing that nothing had changed, he sent down a third to check whether the first two had made it to their destination. A fourth would have followed shortly had he not realized that his cup was now empty and quite useless to him. A bell twinkled nearby and he felt the undeniable urge to look up. Standing at the door was a tall man with a brown overcoat and velvet green bowler hat. Michael slapped his forehead in mental agony and jumped up to chase after the short little Belgian man.
