Issue #7

City Life
A Magazine of Fiction, Science, and a little more Fiction!

Frank awoke. He sat up, checked his watch, and rose to make some tea. While his tea water boiled he stepped over and chose a nice pair of work shoes, slacks, a dress shirt and tie. He grabbed a mug and selected an herbal chai tea. Steeping it for two minutes and forty five seconds, he dunked his head under cold water and combed his hair. He changed, grabbed his tea, and toasted a bagel. In five minutes his bagel would be roasted, buttered, and half eaten as he grabbed his jacket and headed out the door for another day of work in New York City.

Bus stop, bus, building, floor, cubicle, computer. Every day he took the same path, following the same pattern, at the same times in the same places. Lunch came, coworker number three talked about the latest news in the football world, and Frank downed his BLT as he considered whether Vinny would ever catch on that he hated sports. Apparently not. Ellis questioned him on the quality of the company coffee and just generally continued her ever present paranoia of percolated beverages.

Lunch disappeared over the horizon and evening fell with a splat over the city skyline. At exactly five o’clock, Frank looked outside and noticed that once again fifth avenue was backed up with city traffic. Everyone needs the exercise, he thought as the elevator reached the ground floor. Three seconds at stoplight one, ten seconds to cross the second, and if he ran he could reach the third just in time. He didn’t run.

Frank made his way across the city in only twenty one minutes. Varying from his normal walking arrival by 3 seconds, it might even qualify as an eventful day. He hung up his jacket, loosened his tie and took off his shoes. Another day successfully finished. He reheated yesterday's leftovers and clicked on the TV, watching the newest football game as he mentally checked off dinner in his head. When eight o’clock arrived he got up, washed his fork and mug and threw away his styrofoam container, curry residue still sticking to its sides. He set his tie ready for the next day and placed his shoes perpendicular to his dresser. After taking a twenty minute shower, he climbed into bed. Drifting off to sleep around nine, he knew exactly what the next day would hold for him. Or, at least he thought so anyway.

Awaking at seven, Frank got up to boil his tea water. He chose an outfit that included the same tie, but different shoes and a slightly different shade shirt. He washed and combed his hair while mentally deciding on mint as his tea of the day. Variety! Mug in hand, he filled his cup until the tea bag poked just over the top. Bagel roasted and cream cheese spread, he grabbed his jacket as he started another day.

Tech work was considered to be a well paying job with an interesting problem solving dynamic but Frank just found it to be work. His chief motivation was to make money. Why else work? His second was that he wanted to make his parents proud. He had his own apartment, a stable job, friends, and what most people would call a cushy life. His third motivation would more accurately be summed up by describing his lack of direction. Sure, he could do something else, but nothing else presented itself to him. It wasn’t that tech was appealing to him, but it wasn’t distasteful and it kept him alive. Fulfilling for some, but just a job to Frank. His answer to “why do it” was simply “why not?”. He didn’t mind.

Being a company holiday, Frank was forced to change things up by leaving work at lunch. He had the whole afternoon to himself and nothing to do with it. So he made a choice that would make any mother proud. He decided to take a walk through central park. As the elevator reached the ground floor he stepped outside and crossed over Light One, hitting it perfectly, and detoured left into the park. Green and expansive, Frank had always liked coming to the park on the weekends to relax and think. Today, he thought, would just be considered a bonus day. It was Friday after all.

At the entrance of the park, there hung a sign. It read “The Mysterious Loki! Staying near Central park all week! Demonstrations at 1:30”. Today was going to be interesting. Strolling leisurely through the park, Frank decided that he could do with a good bamboozlement. After having reached the center of the park, he sat down choosing a seat near the back and looked around. There were about fifteen guests waiting to see the magician, a decent crowd for somebody nobody had ever heard anything about. Sitting in front of the crowd there was a raised platform about three feet from the ground.

As he waited, Frank decided to look around and enjoy his stay. Trees stood tall and straight, completely blocking any sight of the city. This was part of the reason he liked central park, it was seclusive and felt as if he was in the middle of nowhere as opposed to the concrete jungle he actually inhabited. Contrary to the woodsy feel, a tarmac track wound through the park making it accessible to all kinds of individuals. Convenient for them, but annoying for everyone else. From experience, Frank knew that the path was about six miles long, something he hadn’t known when he first took a walk during his lunch break. Oops. He checked his watch. 1:28. Where would the magician approach from? Wouldn’t they see him if he approached? Maybe he’ll teleport thought Frank as he kept his eyes open for misdirection. The crowd was starting to get restless. A few seated individuals muttered things about being late and another even went so far as to whisper loudly: “Where is this fellow”. Positively rude by Frank’s standards.

“Well, I for one am not waiting any longer.” This came from a man seated in the middle of the spectators. He stood up, walked up the aisle and mosied on up to the stage. Almost like he owned the place. “Time to get this show on the road.” With that the man gave a dramatic spin and turned into a tornado of red and green. Technically he only made one spin, but once he had, his entire outfit had changed. He was now wearing a long black overcoat, white dress shirt, bow tie, top hat, and shiny black salesmen shoes. A scruffy business man had gone from rumpled worker to spiffy magician in only 360 degrees. Frank was interested. A few audience members clapped.

“Thank you. Now, for my first course of action I would like to show you an old and very difficult trick.” The man took off his top hat, showed it to the crowd and continued. “As you can see, this top hat-” he twirled on one finger tip, “-is completely normal. Not the lack of contents inside.” Out of nowhere a short black stick materialized. Whispering the words "Abracadabra" he waved his wand over the hat. Nothing happened. Big surprise thought Frank as he watched in bemusement. Then, with a flourish of his wrist, the showman pulled a fluffy white rabbit out of the hat. “And here we have ourselves a healthy looking rabbit.” The audience clapped, the magician bowed, and Frank squinted.

“Of course, we have all heard of someone pulling a rabbit out of a hat, but have you seen someone put a rabbit back in a hat?” A few audience members raised a quizzical eyebrow. He handed the rabbit to the nearest seated onlooker.“Nevermind. How about a card trick?” Out came a deck of cards. Flying from one hand to another, the cards gracefully arched back and forth, mesmerising the audience. “Right. Lady in the front wearing the blue dress….think of a card, any card. Now, tell it quietly to the people around you. Great, everyone got it?” A few audience members nodded their heads amiably. Frank certainly hadn’t got it. “Is your card, perhaps, the queen of clubs?” He said this last part in a victorious crescendo. Even Frank clapped. After a few more renditions of the same thing, the Mysterious Loki decided to move on: “How about an illusion?”

“Any body feeling like being sawn in half?” The crowd shuffled nervously. “No? Ah, well my licence for that one had been revoked anyway. The best tricks don’t rely on licensing. Time, as we all know, flows ever onward. It can neither be cornered nor corralled. It is…emperial, potent, dare I say sovereign? I thought so too at one point. Who would like to experience time in a….shall we say “unique” fashion?”

Nobody stood up. A few men coughed nervously. A phone rang. Didn’t they know they were supposed to silence them? Rude. Eventually the silence became so unbearable that Frank stood up. It was only a stage act anyway, what could a fake-magician really do? “I’ll do it.” His voice rang clearly over the silent crowd.

“Very good! Please approach the stage. Now, have you ever wondered how Bill Murray felt while filming “ground hog day”? Well, today is your lucky day because not only will I give you this opportunity, I will give it to you for free.” Frank stood directly in front of the magician in a stoically resigned stance. “All you have to do is sit back down at your seat after I wave my wand over you three times and mutter the word: “presto”. Ready?” He waved the wand once, twice, three times, and like a practiced ventriloquist the word “presto” bound from no particular mouth out across the park. Frank went back to his seat and sat down feeling a mix of dread and relief. See? Nothing to worry about. Or was that what he was supposed to think? Thoughts like these passed through his head as he suddenly wished he hadn’t tried to acquire the extra exercise.

“Thank you sir for your time. You should reset when you sit down at your seat about the time I finish my next act. Moving on, an age-old trick for any magician is the disappearance of an object or person. This man just let a strangely clad showman experiment with time on him, surely one of you will allow me to make you invisible.” Three hands in the audience shot up in reply. Apparently messing with the visible world isn’t as nerve racking as messing with the temporal.

“Up to the stage please. That’s it. One at a time, ladies first. Presto!” The first lady on stage disappeared with a wave of a wand. Right as the second woman was going to disappear with a cry and a wave, the entire proceeding ground to a halt as two overweight individuals came roaring by on some kind of powered wheel chair. They were rocketing around turns so quickly that they seemed to be in some kind of race. Not much of a noon time stroll. “Presto!!!” Yelled the magician and the second lady disappeared. As the third was a rather large man with much to make disappear, the trickster waved his wand in a figure eight pattern and muttered something indecipherable in addition to presto. After this he went on to make certain objects in the audience’s possession materialise in his possession. Then he made some trinkets disappear and ended with the return of the three people he had made disappear.

“And this concludes my third——Thank you sir for your time. You should reset when you sit down at your seat about the time I finish my next act. Moving on, an age-old trick for any magician is the disappearance of an object or person.” Something was definitely wrong. Did the magician run out of script? Why was he repeating himself? As if everything was normal, he continued: “This man just let a strangely clad showman experiment with time on him, surely one of you will allow me to make you invisible.” As if everything was normal, three hands shot up. Two women and a man.

Up to the stage if you please. Ladies first and one at a time. That’s it. Right. Presto!!” The first lady disappeared. Ignoring the magic show, Frank looked around. A biker slogged by traveling at a painfully slow speed. It went out of sight as two maniac racers careened around a corner loudly disrupting the whole event. What Frank hadn’t noticed before was the crash that echoed loudly from the biker’s and racer’s direction. Ouch.

“Presto!!” The man disappeared. Frank stuck up his hand. “Yes, Mr………”

“Frank. Frank Williams. Have you done this before?”

“I would have you know that I have made thirty-two men and women disappear. If you are doubting my ability to bring them back however, I must say that I have only brought thirty back.” With a wave of his wand and a mysterious looking eyebrow movement three people appeared exactly where they had disappeared. “Confidence restored I hope. Now, as we all know, objects have a way of wandering off. Sometimes you forget you have keys in your pocket and when you remember, they’re gone. Often your phone will decide that it wants to be in an arm chair rather than the desk you left it on. These things happen at random and anyone could tell you that it’s not magic. Making them disappear when you want them to however, is. Mrs. Blue, is this your purse?”

A tall lady in a blue dress patted herself confusedly. “Yes, I–I think it is.” He tossed it to her.

“What about you, Mr. Green? Could you possibly be missing a watch?” He dangled a large crystalline watch from his gloved hand. Indeed Mr. Green had unwittingly lost his watch. Mrs. Brown had lost three pairs of handkerchiefs and Mrs. Green one cellphone.

It was about this time that Frank was starting to think that the magician may actually be a wizard. Though his lack of admission to Frank’s possible time loop left him confused, it didn’t rule out any possibilities. If he was a real magician (an idea that Frank did not take lightly) he would have declared to his whole audience that Frank traveled through time. If he was a fake magician then Frank shouldn’t be having such déjà-vu and the showman wouldn’t have decided to try a trick he knew was impossible. If he was using technology, Frank thought, he would have been recruited by the government long ago and never gone on the road as a magician. Strange dilemma.

Frank looked up to realize that the magician was talking to him about sitting down. Was he just crazy? Could he be suffering from some suggested hallucination? Maybe the illusionist was making him think that he was looping when really he was just standing on the stage unconscious and drooling? Impossible. Frank didn’t drool.

‘vroooooooom!!!!!’

Halfway, thought Frank. Odd,--there was no biker to crash into this time. Frank was starting to get a little worried about now and decided that he wished he was drinking some sort of tea. Anything for his hands to keep moving! He checked his watch. Why hadn’t he earlier? 2:00. Huh. No time had passed. This sunk it for Frank and he started hyperventilating. Maybe he should take a walk. No! That’s what got him here in the first place! Frank stood up in a rush and only after realized that he had bumped into someone.

“Watch out man!” Muttered a green coated gentleman who was checking his wrist for a watch. He addressed the stage artist next: “I assume that that is my watch?” After having his watch returned and being complimented on his good taste he sat back down in contemplation.

Frank copied his movement. Trickster or not, he was good. His transitions between past and present were seamless. No strange jumps, blurry vision, or desire for dairy products. It seemed like a natural progression, which ruled out technology as a possible hypothesis. How long was the loop? Deciding to time the next run, Frank started to calm down. Whether it was real or false, no harm was being done to him.

Somebody bumped into him—Mr. Green was standing up again—and Frank was reminded to check his watch. 2:20. If the magician was a fake, he couldn’t control the people around him and they would act differently each rerun. Heck they’d be incensed by now only ever seeing the same thing! Why wasn’t Frank?

“Thank you sir, you may sit down now……”

Wait, had he said it exactly like that before? Frank’s eyes squinted as he mulled over the words. Nothing had changed on his part, so why should the “Mysterious Loki’s” words change? Was he a fake or was the magician breaking out of the loop himself?

Nevermind. Frank had had enough! Abruptly he stood up and shouted at his torturer: “you know what?! No! I’ve had enough. I don’t know what you’ve done, or how you’ve done it, but whatever you did, I’m leaving!”

With that he spun round and stalked towards the tarmac path. Furriously running his hands through his hair, Frank didn’t realize that he was speeding up. Soon he was running, legs pumping as he tried to clear his mind. Time! Disappear! Words kept running through his head in a loop, each time belted out by that phoney stage artist. What time was it anyway??? 2:10.

Frank slipped. He fell. His heart skipped a beat as he realized this fact. Unfortunately gravity had realized the fact as well and was quickly taking advantage of his inertia. Dirt flew past his eyes as he crash-landed onto the lawn beside the path. Oh! Thank God! Grass is significantly nicer than asphalt as any even mediocre jogger knows.

Slowly getting his hands underneath him, Frank raised himself into a sitting position. Ow. Everything ached, but nothing could possibly quench the burning understanding that his watch only read 2:11. Renewed with strength Frank leapt to his feat, an action that wouldn’t seem possible after his previous tumble. Though limping slightly, he quickly got his rhythm back as he jogged onward, completing the entire route without even noticing his exhaustion. A strange sight for any innocent bystander, trousers and suit jacket all covered in grass stains and a rip near the hem of his left pant leg.

But there they were. The view from behind the stage revealed a lot. There were flaps beneath the contraption, clearly used as trapdoors to hide a person’s exit. Even stranger was the fact that all the people were packing up, not leaving, but all pitching in to clean everything up.

“My suspicions exactly,” muttered Frank as he casually walked on by the stage performers, “the magician wasn’t acting alone….”

Still, it begged the question. Why was Frank bothered by a little repetition? Wasn’t that basically what his entire life was? Wake up. Eat. Go to work. Five o'clock arrives, he walks home, watches football, eats, watches the ads, showers, and goes to sleep. Frank in a nutshell. Frank let out a long pent up sigh. Maybe I should do something about that.
