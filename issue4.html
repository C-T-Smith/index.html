Jam, an Exercise in Dialogue

“Well,” said Jim, “I’m not so sure you should be making such a hasty conclusion.”

His listener, a portly man of a piggish disposition, stood across from him looking entirely unpersuaded.

“On the contrary, my belief has always been to go with my gut. Instinct, that's the way for me.”

“Perhaps,” continued Jim, “but have you considered the fact that strawberries have always been the jam for the perfect luncheon? To go with grape jelly now would only break tradition.”

The two men stood contemplatively, staring at the aisle shelves in thought. One short round, and balding, the other tall, thin, and with a full head of hair. They may have been the complete opposite of each other by physical standards, but as they had only just begun to realize in the few seconds that they had known each other, they were also the opposite in preference. They were however the same in their philosophical disposition regarding such mundane topics as jam.

“I disagree,” retorted the short man, grabbing a jar of smuckers grape jelly in his puggy hand. “A little change can be a good thing. Eat the same thing too much and you’ll never advance your sense of taste.”

“That is of course, assuming that you should change your preferences. Why change your preferences on a whim?” Jim reached for another jar of grape jelly, peering at the extensive list near the nutritional facts page. “Take the ingredients for example. Sodium Benzoate. What the heck is that?”

“Some form of salt, I presume.”

“Maybe, but do you really want to be forcing your body to digest that? Why don’t they just use real salt? And potassium sorbate. We see it everywhere but have no idea what it really is. Some form of potassium, but that doesn’t really help.”

“I concede that grape jelly may not be the most healthy, but” –the portly man grabbed a jar of strawberry jam as he spoke– “what about the ingredients of tradition? Pectin? What, pray tell, is the benefit to eating pig hooves rather than off-brand salt? Additionally, the jam has both the questionable salt and the potassium that the jelly has with the bonus of hoof. Not to mention whatever substance they include to look like the whole strawberry part of the ‘strawberry’ jam.”

Eyeing the jar suspiciously he set it carefully back on the shelf and looked across at his verbal sparring partner with a question in his eyes. Sourly, Jim spun the jar of grape jelly in his hand and set it next to the strawberry.

“The grape has pectin too…” was all he could muster before turning his mind elsewhere.

“That opens an entirely different can of worms,” The short man sniffed, “but I see your point.”

With that he spun and snatched up a bag of bread that was conveniently placed on the same aisle. He looked back at Jim, eyes daring him to say something. Both silently agreed to leave the glutinous material out of the debate, finally reaching a point they could agree on. Jim chose the loaf next to his and turned to leave.

“Where are you going?” called out his new acquaintance.

“For a jar of peanut butter and a bunch of bananas.”

The man’s stomach rumbled pleasantly as Jim left. Bananas ay? Couldn’t really argue with that… always go with your gut, that was his rule. He headed the opposite direction, seeking the produce aisle.
